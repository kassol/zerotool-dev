<h2>How to Use</h2>
<ol>
  <li>Enter your <strong>Password</strong> in the key field.</li>
  <li>To <strong>Encrypt</strong>: paste your plaintext in the left panel and click <strong>Encrypt</strong>. The base64-encoded ciphertext appears on the right.</li>
  <li>To <strong>Decrypt</strong>: paste the ciphertext in the right panel and click <strong>Decrypt</strong>. The plaintext appears on the left.</li>
  <li>Click <strong>Copy</strong> to copy the result.</li>
</ol>

<h2>Ciphertext Format</h2>
<p>New ciphertext is <code>v2:</code> followed by Base64 of <code>salt (16 bytes) | nonce (12 bytes) | ciphertext + tag (16 bytes)</code>. The key is derived with PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256 at 600,000 iterations, the value in the <a href="https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Password_Storage_Cheat_Sheet.html#pbkdf2" rel="noopener">OWASP Password Storage Cheat Sheet</a>.</p>
<p>Ciphertext made with this tool before 2026-09-30 has no prefix and used 200,000 iterations. Paste it as it is: the tool sees that there is no prefix and decrypts it with 200,000 iterations. To move it to the new format, decrypt it and encrypt it again.</p>

<h2>Limitations</h2>
<ul>
  <li>Programs written for the old format do not read <code>v2:</code> ciphertext. Remove the prefix and use 600,000 iterations.</li>
  <li>The tool encrypts text (UTF-8), not files.</li>
  <li>PBKDF2 makes each password guess slower, but a short or common password can still be guessed. Use a long random passphrase.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Security Notes</h2>
<p>AES-256-GCM is computationally secure against brute-force attacks. However, the encryption is only as strong as your password. Use a long, random passphrase for sensitive data. The authentication tag in GCM means decryption will fail loudly if the ciphertext is tampered with.</p>

<h2>What Is AES?</h2>
<p>AES (Advanced Encryption Standard) is the symmetric encryption algorithm standardized by NIST in 2001. It secures HTTPS traffic, encrypted filesystems, and password managers worldwide. AES-256 uses a 256-bit key and 14 rounds of transformation per block.</p>
