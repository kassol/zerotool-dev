<h2>How Words Are Split</h2>
<p>camelCase, PascalCase, kebab-case, snake_case and CONSTANT_CASE first split the input into words, with the same rules as the <a href="https://github.com/blakeembrey/change-case" rel="noopener">change-case</a> library's <code>split</code> with <code>separateNumbers</code>:</p>
<ul>
  <li>At a lowercase letter or digit followed by an uppercase letter: <code>userLoginCount</code> → user, Login, Count.</li>
  <li>Before the last capital of an acronym that starts a new word: <code>XMLHttpRequest</code> → XML, Http, Request.</li>
  <li>Between letters and digits: <code>base64Encode</code> → base, 64, Encode.</li>
  <li>At any run of spaces, punctuation or symbols: <code>user-login_count</code> → user, login, count.</li>
</ul>
<p>Apostrophes are removed before splitting (as lodash does), so <code>don't stop</code> → <code>dontStop</code>. Letters from any script and their accents are kept: <code>café au lait</code> → <code>caféAuLait</code>, <code>Привет мир</code> → <code>приветМир</code>.</p>

<h2>Limitations</h2>
<ul>
  <li>Title Case and Sentence case keep your spacing and punctuation and do not split identifiers: <code>userLoginCount</code> → <code>Userlogincount</code>. Title Case capitalizes every word, including short words such as "of" and "the".</li>
  <li>Acronyms are not kept in capitals in camelCase or PascalCase: <code>parseHTML</code> → <code>parseHtml</code>.</li>
  <li>Case changes use the browser's default Unicode rules, not a language-specific locale: <code>ß</code> becomes <code>SS</code> in CONSTANT_CASE, and Turkish dotted/dotless i is not handled.</li>
  <li>Scripts without letter case (Chinese, Japanese, Korean) pass through unchanged; only spaces and punctuation between them become separators.</li>
</ul>
