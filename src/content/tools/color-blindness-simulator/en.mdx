<h2>How to Use</h2>
<ol>
  <li>In <strong>Color mode</strong>, enter a hex color or use the color picker. The tool instantly shows how that color appears under each of the 8 color vision deficiency types in a comparison grid.</li>
  <li>In <strong>Image mode</strong>, drag and drop an image (or click to browse). The tool processes the image locally and displays 9 side-by-side previews — the original plus all 8 simulations.</li>
  <li>Move the <strong>severity</strong> slider (0.1–0.9) to change how strong protanomaly, deuteranomaly and tritanomaly are. Protanopia, deuteranopia and tritanopia are always severity 1.0.</li>
  <li>In Color mode, click any hex value to copy it to your clipboard.</li>
</ol>

<h2>Understanding Color Vision Deficiency</h2>
<p>Color vision deficiency (CVD) affects how the eye's cone cells respond to light. Human color vision relies on three types of cone cells: L-cones (red), M-cones (green), and S-cones (blue). When one type is missing or malfunctioning, certain colors become indistinguishable.</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Protanopia / Protanomaly</strong> — affects L-cones (red perception). Reds appear darker and shift toward brown or green. About 2% of males.</li>
  <li><strong>Deuteranopia / Deuteranomaly</strong> — affects M-cones (green perception). The most common form, affecting roughly 6% of males. Greens and reds look similar.</li>
  <li><strong>Tritanopia / Tritanomaly</strong> — affects S-cones (blue perception). Rare, affecting under 0.01% of the population. Blues and greens become confused.</li>
  <li><strong>Achromatopsia / Achromatomaly</strong> — affects all cone types. Complete achromatopsia results in grayscale vision; achromatomaly significantly reduces color saturation.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Design Tips for Accessibility</h2>
<p>Use this tool alongside the <a href="/tools/color-contrast-checker/">Color Contrast Checker</a> for a complete accessibility audit. Rely on shape, pattern, or text labels rather than color alone to convey meaning. Test critical UI elements — error states, charts, status indicators — through at least protanopia and deuteranopia simulations, as these cover the majority of affected users.</p>

<h2>Privacy</h2>
<p>All simulation runs in your browser. No images or color data are sent to any server.</p>

<h2>Limits</h2>
<ul>
  <li>The protan, deutan and tritan types use the <a href="https://www.inf.ufrgs.br/~oliveira/pubs_files/CVD_Simulation/CVD_Simulation.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Machado et al. (2009)</a> matrices in linear RGB, the same pipeline as the R package <a href="https://colorspace.r-forge.r-project.org/reference/simulate_cvd.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener">colorspace</a> with <code>linear = TRUE</code>. Results can differ from tools that use the Brettel (1997) or Viénot (1999) models.</li>
  <li>Severity moves in the paper's steps of 0.1. Achromatopsia is the sRGB (Rec. 709) luminance in linear light. Achromatomaly has no published matrix: the tool mixes the original and the achromatopsia result 50/50 in linear light.</li>
  <li>Images larger than 600 pixels on either side are scaled down to fit 600 × 600 before simulation, so small text in a full-page screenshot may blur. Crop the area you want to check first.</li>
  <li>Color mode accepts 3- or 6-digit hex values only.</li>
</ul>
