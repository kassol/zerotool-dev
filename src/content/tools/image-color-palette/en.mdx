<h2>What this does</h2>

<p>Drop in any picture — a UI mockup, a brand asset, a product photograph, a piece of stock art — and walk away with a palette that is ready to paste into your stylesheet, design tokens, or Tailwind theme. The tool clusters the visible pixels with k-means and reports each cluster as a swatch with its weight in the source image, plus four color-space readouts: HEX for design tools, RGB and HSL for legacy CSS, and OKLCH for modern color pipelines.</p>

<h2>How to use</h2>

<ol>
  <li>Drop an image onto the upload card, click the card to pick a file, or paste with `Ctrl + V` after copying an image to your clipboard.</li>
  <li>Tweak the controls: pick how many colors to extract (3 to 16), sort the result by frequency, hue, or lightness, and toggle filters that drop transparent pixels or near-white pixels.</li>
  <li>Click any swatch to copy its HEX value. Click a row inside the swatch to copy that specific format — RGB, HSL, or OKLCH.</li>
  <li>Use <strong>Copy CSS variables</strong> for a `:root { --color-1: …; }` block, <strong>Copy JSON</strong> for an array of `{hex, rgb, weight}`, or <strong>Copy Tailwind</strong> for a `theme.extend.colors.palette` snippet.</li>
</ol>

<h2>About the algorithm</h2>

<p>K-means is the simplest clustering method that still produces designer-friendly palettes. The picture is downsampled so the longest edge is 200 pixels — a 3840×2160 photograph becomes 200×113, about 22 600 sample pixels. Each pixel is assigned to the centroid with the smallest squared-RGB distance, centroids are recomputed as the mean of their cluster, and the loop runs until centroids stop moving (or 24 iterations, whichever happens first).</p>

<p>Two practical refinements are layered on top: tiny clusters that drift to within ΔRGB &lt; 6 of another are merged so the output never shows duplicate-looking swatches, and the optional pre-filters drop transparent pixels and near-white pixels (luminance &gt; 0.95) so the algorithm focuses on the colors that carry visual weight rather than padding around the subject.</p>

<h2>Example: A Banner with a Small Logo</h2>

<p>A 400×300 PNG: a navy <code>#1E3A8A</code> background, a near-white <code>#F8FAFC</code> strip across the bottom quarter, and a 60×60 orange <code>#F97316</code> square (3% of the area). With <strong>Colors</strong> set to 3, sorted by frequency:</p>

<ul>
  <li>Run 1: <code>#1F3B8B</code> 72.3%, <code>#F8FAFC</code> 24.7%, <code>#F97316</code> 3.0%.</li>
  <li>Run 2 (Re-extract): <code>#1E3A8A</code> 71.7%, <code>#F5F7FB</code> 25.3%, <code>#F97316</code> 3.0%.</li>
  <li><strong>Skip near-white</strong> on: <code>#1E3A8A</code> 95.1%, <code>#F97316</code> 4.0%, <code>#8B9AC3</code> 0.9%.</li>
  <li>Colors set to 8: only four swatches, the three source colors plus <code>#8B9AC3</code> 0.7%.</li>
</ul>

<p>The two runs differ because the starting centroids are picked at random, and the hex values move by one or two steps when a centroid absorbs some of the blended pixels along the color edges. <code>#8B9AC3</code> is such a blend: scaling the image down to 200×150 mixes the navy and near-white rows where they meet. Asking for 8 colors returns 4 because empty clusters are dropped and centroids closer than 6 (summed RGB difference) are merged. With near-white skipped, the weights are shares of the remaining pixels, not of the whole image.</p>

<h2>Limits</h2>

<ul>
  <li>Results can change between runs; copy the palette you like before clicking Re-extract.</li>
  <li>A small accent can be absorbed into a larger neighboring cluster when its color is close, so it may not appear until you raise the number of colors.</li>
  <li>Colors are averaged in sRGB, so a centroid can be a slightly duller mix of the pixels it represents rather than an exact source color.</li>
  <li>Skip near-white drops pixels whose luminance, computed from the gamma-encoded RGB values, is above 0.95. Skip transparent drops pixels with alpha below 128.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Output formats</h2>

<ul>
  <li><strong>HEX</strong> — the universal interop format. Click the colored top of any swatch to copy.</li>
  <li><strong>RGB</strong> — modern space-separated syntax (`rgb(160 90 44)`), compatible with every evergreen browser.</li>
  <li><strong>HSL</strong> — handy when you want to nudge hue, saturation, or lightness without leaving the swatch.</li>
  <li><strong>OKLCH</strong> — perceptually uniform, ideal for design tokens and color pipelines that need to mix or rotate hues without surprises.</li>
  <li><strong>Weight %</strong> — the share of source pixels each centroid claimed; use the dominant entry as a brand accent and the smaller ones as supporting tones.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Privacy</h2>

<p>The tool runs entirely client-side. The image is decoded into an HTMLImageElement, drawn onto a hidden canvas at the sampling size, and clustered in JavaScript. Nothing about the picture is sent to any server. If you would like to verify, open the network tab and watch a clean log every time you load a new image.</p>
