## How to Use

1. Drop an image on the drop zone, click to choose one, or paste a screenshot with **Ctrl/Cmd+V**.
2. Pick a mode:
   - **Grid**: set rows and columns (1 to 20 each).
   - **Tile size**: set the tile width and height in pixels, plus margin and spacing for sprite sheets.
   - **Instagram**: choose *Profile grid* (3 columns, 1 to 10 rows) or *Carousel* (2 to 20 slides in one row), the post size, and *Crop* or *Pad*.
3. Check the preview. Cut lines and tile numbers are drawn on the image. In Instagram crop mode, drag the highlighted box (or focus it and use the arrow keys) to choose the part of the image that goes into the posts.
4. Choose **PNG**, **JPG** or **WebP**. JPG and WebP have a quality slider; JPG also has a background color for transparent areas.
5. Click **Download ZIP**, or the download icon under a tile to save just that tile.

## What You Can Do With It

- **Instagram profile grid**: split one wide or tall picture into 3, 6 or 9 posts that form a single image on your profile.
- **Seamless carousel**: turn a panorama into slides that line up edge to edge as people swipe.
- **Print tiling**: cut a poster into A4-sized pieces with a 2 × 3 or 3 × 4 grid, print each one and tape them together.
- **Sprite sheets**: cut a sheet of game frames or icons back into single images, with margin and spacing handled.
- **Long screenshots**: split a full-page capture into parts that fit a slide, a chat message or a ticket.

## Equal Splits Without Losing Pixels

Most images do not divide evenly. A 4000 px wide photo split into 3 columns gives 1333.33 px per column. Rounding every tile down to 1333 px drops the last pixel column; rounding up makes tiles overlap. The grid mode places the cut lines at `round(i × width / columns)` instead, so the 3 columns become 1333, 1334 and 1333 px wide. Tile sizes differ by at most 1 px, every source pixel lands in exactly one tile, and grid and tile-size output keeps the original resolution with no scaling.

Instagram tiles are different: each one is scaled to 1080 px wide (1440, 1350 or 1080 px tall). If your crop area is narrower than 1080 px per column, the tool enlarges it and shows how much, so you know before posting that the result will be soft.

## Instagram Grid and Carousel Details

In **Crop** mode, the tool picks the largest area that fits inside your image and has the ratio of the finished set (3240:4320 for a 3 × 3 grid of 3:4 posts). You move that area; its size stays at the maximum. In **Pad** mode the whole image is fitted into the target and the empty space is filled with a color you choose, which is useful for tall screenshots or logos that must not be cut.

Instagram shows a thin gap between grid posts. The tiles are cut edge to edge, so a thin line of the picture sits under each gap. For most photos this is not visible.

## File Names

Tiles are named after the original file, so they sort correctly in any file manager:

| Mode | Example |
|---|---|
| Grid, Tile size | `poster-r1-c1.png`, `poster-r1-c2.png` … (row and column, 1-based) |
| Instagram profile grid | `trip-post-1.jpg` … `trip-post-9.jpg` (posting order) |
| Instagram carousel | `pano-slide-1.jpg` … `pano-slide-5.jpg` (left to right) |

Numbers are zero-padded to the digit count of the total, so `post-01` sorts before `post-10`. Pasted images use `image` as the name.

## Limits

- One image at a time. Animated GIFs load as their first frame; use the [GIF Splitter](/tools/gif-splitter/) to take every frame apart.
- Joining images into one sheet is the reverse job. Use the [Sprite Sheet Generator](/tools/sprite-sheet-generator/) for that.
- At most 400 tiles, a 20 × 20 grid, and a source image of 50 million pixels (16,384 px per side).
- Output is PNG, JPG or WebP. Browsers that cannot encode WebP (older Safari) save PNG instead, and the status line says so.
