## What You Can Do With It

- **Instagram profile grid**: split one wide or tall picture into 3, 6 or 9 posts that form a single image on your profile.
- **Seamless carousel**: turn a panorama into slides that line up edge to edge as people swipe.
- **Print tiling**: cut a poster into A4-sized pieces with a 2 × 3 or 3 × 4 grid, print each one and tape them together.
- **Sprite sheets**: cut a sheet of game frames or icons back into single images, with margin and spacing handled.
- **Long screenshots**: split a full-page capture into parts that fit a slide, a chat message or a ticket.

## Equal Splits Without Losing Pixels

Most images do not divide evenly. A 4000 px wide photo split into 3 columns gives 1333.33 px per column. Rounding every tile down to 1333 px drops the last pixel column; rounding up makes tiles overlap. The grid mode places the cut lines at `round(i × width / columns)` instead, so the 3 columns become 1333, 1334 and 1333 px wide. Tile sizes differ by at most 1 px, every source pixel lands in exactly one tile, and grid and tile-size output keeps the original resolution with no scaling.

Instagram tiles are different: each one is scaled to 1080 px wide (1440, 1350 or 1080 px tall). If your crop area is narrower than 1080 px per column, the tool enlarges it and shows how much, so you know before posting that the result will be soft.

## Instagram Grid and Carousel Details

In **Crop** mode, the tool picks the largest area that fits inside your image and has the ratio of the finished set (3240:4320 for a 3 × 3 grid of 3:4 posts). You move that area; its size stays at the maximum. In **Pad** mode the whole image is fitted into the target and the empty space is filled with a color you choose, which is useful for tall screenshots or logos that must not be cut.

Instagram shows a thin gap between grid posts. The tiles are cut edge to edge, so a thin line of the picture sits under each gap. For most photos this is not visible.

## Worked Examples

These numbers come from the tool's own layout functions.

- **Cut lines.** A 4000 px wide image in 3 columns is cut at 0, 1333, 2667 and 4000 (tiles 1333, 1334 and 1333 px). A 4001 px image is cut at 0, 1334, 2667 and 4001 (1334, 1333, 1334). The extra pixels are spread across the row, not piled into the last tile.
- **Posting order.** For a 3 × 3 profile grid, the posting numbers on the preview are:

  ```text
  9  8  7
  6  5  4
  3  2  1
  ```

  The tile in row `r` and column `c` (counted from 0) gets `(rows − 1 − r) × 3 + (2 − c) + 1`.
- **When the posts come out soft.** A 3 × 3 set of 3:4 posts is 3240 × 4320 px. From a 4000 × 3000 landscape photo, the largest crop of that shape is 2250 × 3000, so the tiles are enlarged 1.44 times. The same photo as one row of three 3:4 posts (3240 × 1440) gives a 4000 × 1778 crop, which is scaled down and stays sharp.
- **Carousel.** Five 4:5 slides form a 5400 × 1350 strip, a 4:1 shape; a 12000 × 3000 panorama fills it with no crop.
- **Sprite sheet.** With tile size 32 × 32, margin 2 and spacing 1, the frames in each row start at x = 2, 35, 68 and 101. The same terms are used by the map editor Tiled in its [tileset format](https://doc.mapeditor.org/en/stable/reference/tmx-map-format/).

## Common Problems

- **The grid looks shifted after you post.** Every single post you add later moves all tiles one cell and breaks the rows; post in multiples of three to keep them aligned. Pinned posts sit at the top-left before your newest posts (Instagram allows up to three, [The Verge, June 2022](https://www.theverge.com/2022/6/7/23158443/instagram-profile-pin-three-posts-reels-tiktok-twitter)); unpin them before you check the result.
- **The 3:4 grid ratio.** Instagram moved profile grids from squares to rectangles in January 2025 ([The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/2025/1/17/24346304/instagram-profile-grids-rectangles-squares)), and reports describe the tile as 3:4. We found no Instagram help page that states the ratio, so if a publishing tool refuses a 3:4 file, split again at 4:5.
- **A script gives different tiles than this page.** Python 3 rounds halves to even: `round(2.5)` is 2, while JavaScript's `Math.round(2.5)` is 3. For 5 px in 2 columns, the middle cut is at 2 in Python and 3 here. Integer arithmetic, `(2 × i × L + n) // (2 × n)`, matches `Math.round`.
- **ffmpeg drops a pixel row.** For JPEGs decoded with 4:2:0 chroma subsampling, ffmpeg's [`crop` filter](https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#crop) rounds size and position down to even values. In our test with ffmpeg 9.0.2, a 1334 × 1001 crop came out 1334 × 1000; adding `exact=1` gave 1334 × 1001.
- **Phone photos come out sideways in other tools.** The browser applies the EXIF orientation when it decodes the image ([MDN: createImageBitmap](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Window/createImageBitmap)), so tiles here come out upright. Pillow does not; call `ImageOps.exif_transpose` first. ImageMagick needs `-auto-orient`.
- **Thin tiles at the right or bottom edge of a sprite sheet.** Margin applies at the top and left only. Turn on *Drop partial edge tiles*, or *Skip fully transparent tiles* when the border is transparent.

## File Names

Tiles are named after the original file, so they sort correctly in any file manager:

| Mode | Example |
|---|---|
| Grid, Tile size | `poster-r1-c1.png`, `poster-r1-c2.png` … (row and column, 1-based) |
| Instagram profile grid | `trip-post-1.jpg` … `trip-post-9.jpg` (posting order) |
| Instagram carousel | `pano-slide-1.jpg` … `pano-slide-5.jpg` (left to right) |

Numbers are zero-padded to the digit count of the total, so `post-01` sorts before `post-10`. Pasted images use `image` as the name.

## Limits

- One image at a time. Animated GIFs load as their first frame; use the [GIF Splitter](/tools/gif-splitter/) to take every frame apart.
- Joining images into one sheet is the reverse job. Use the [Sprite Sheet Generator](/tools/sprite-sheet-generator/) for that.
- At most 400 tiles, a 20 × 20 grid, and a source image of 50 million pixels (16,384 px per side).
- Output is PNG, JPG or WebP. Browsers that cannot encode WebP (older Safari) save PNG instead, and the status line says so.
